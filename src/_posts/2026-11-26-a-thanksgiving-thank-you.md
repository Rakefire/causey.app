Thanksgiving opens a season that pulls in two directions. There's the noise of the sales and the countdown to the holidays. And there's the part that matters, when people stop to say thank you and give generously to the causes they care about. Giving Tuesday is next week, and for many of you, the weeks between now and New Year's are some of the most important of the year.

I want to start with the part that matters, and say thank you.

## **Thank You for Staying With Us**

As of this month, 87 organizations pay for Causey. Here's how long they've been with us:

- 33 of them, about 38%, have been with us for four years or more.
- 22, about one in four, have been with us for five years or more.
- 15 have been with us for ten years or more, since long before we were called Causey.

Our longest-standing customer started with us in April 2015, about eleven and a half years ago. Another reaches ten years next week, on December 1.

Most of that ten-year group are Small Business Development Centers, who have been with us since 2016. Today we work with eight SBDC networks in five states, which together run about 80 service centers helping small businesses start and grow.

And some newer relationships have grown in ways I'm proud of. Thrive Alliance has been with us since 2021, and now leads one of the [two nonprofit cohorts we welcomed in October](/welcome-to-causey-celebrating-two-new-nonprofit-cohorts/).

Staying with a tool that long is a choice, and I don't take it for granted. Thank you for trusting us with your plans, for telling us when something wasn't working, and for the patience you've shown while we improved it.

## **A Season to Slow Down**

The holidays are also a reminder to slow down. Most of the year, a strategic plan is something you push forward a little at a time, between board meetings and program deadlines. It's rare to step back and look at the whole year.

These last weeks of the year are a good time to do that. You can look back at what your team actually did, close out this chapter, and start thinking about what next year should look like.

## **Close Out the Year in Your Plan**

If you'd like a place to start, here's what I'd suggest:

- **Score your metrics.** Record the last measurements of the year, so the numbers show where you actually finished.
- **Post a final progress update on each goal.** A status and a sentence or two is enough. Future you, and your board, will be glad to have it.
- **Close out the plan, or this version of it.** If the plan ends this year, an administrator can lock it once the final updates are in, so it stays as a record of what you did. If it carries into next year, treat this as the end of a chapter, and consider cloning it as the starting point for the next one.
- **Take stock, and write it down.** Which goals moved and which stalled? What would you do differently? Capture what you learned while it's fresh, so it can shape the plan that comes next.

None of this has to be a big production. An hour with your leadership team and the plan open on the screen will cover most of it.

## **Then Get Ready to Set Direction**

January fills up fast. Whether you plan now or run a short planning process in the first weeks of the new year, aim to start the year knowing where you're headed. The reflection you do in December makes that work go faster.

From all of us at Causey, Happy Thanksgiving. Thank you for the work you do in your communities, and for letting us be a small part of it.
