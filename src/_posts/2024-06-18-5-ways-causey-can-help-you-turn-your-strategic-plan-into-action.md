Your team spent hours on a detailed strategic plan, and now it sits on a shelf. If that sounds familiar, you're not alone. Many mission-driven organizations get stuck once it's time to put the plan to work, and they tend to trip over the same few things.

Jamie Yazdani of Yazdani Consulting and Ricky Chilcott, co-founder of Mission Met, talked through those problems in a recent LinkedIn Live. This post recaps what they covered and how to fix each one.

If you'd rather watch or listen, [check it out on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aLPIhXWYFnI).

Ricky named five common mistakes teams make when they carry out a plan. Here they are, with some ways to solve them.

**Problem: Being overly ambitious without adequate resources.**

**Solution:** Set goals you can reach with the people and money you have. A consultant can help, or you can just be careful about it while you plan. Ricky also said to track progress over time and to change the plan when you learn something along the way.

Review, revise and track on a regular schedule. You'll learn what your team can really take on and which times of year are busy for strategic work. You'll also know when to push and when to pull back.

Causey has a goal measurement feature, so you can check progress on a regular schedule.

**Problem: Creating overly complex plans.**

**Solution:** Write a plan people can read and understand. That takes real work. Keep trimming it until everyone on the team gets it. People who understand the plan are more likely to use it and pitch in.

Simple doesn't mean "dumbed down." It means the plan is easy to follow and easy to act on. Lead with your top priorities, cut the jargon and break big ideas into steps people can manage. When the team understands the plan, they're more willing to own their part of it.

If your organization faces hard problems, take them on a few at a time instead of all at once. A narrow focus is easier to manage, and you're more likely to succeed.

Causey uses a simple two-section format to keep your plan short and focused. It also has a place for your Focus Areas, Goals and Action Items, and for who is assigned to what.

**Problem: Lack of team engagement in the planning process.**

**Solution:** Bring your whole team into planning from the start. Ricky pointed out that people are more likely to back a plan, and work on it, if they helped write it. When staff help shape your goals and strategies, you hear more points of view. More people also feel the plan belongs to them.

Causey makes it easy to work on the plan together. Everyone on the team can see it, and they can look at it in different ways, such as presentation view and timeline view.

**Problem: Absence of champions to drive the process forward.**

**Solution:** Name champions for the plan as a whole and for each goal. A champion makes sure everyone can find the plan, that people use it, and that it stays on track. Pick people who care about the goals and what they'll change. They'll keep others involved, share progress, deal with problems as they come up and cheer the wins. Over time, that builds a habit of holding each other accountable.

In Causey, you can assign champions to the overall plan, focus areas, goals, metrics and action items. Causey then sends them automated emails. These remind them of milestones and ask them for updates.

**Problem: Failure to track progress over time.**

**Solution:** Set a steady rhythm for reviewing and measuring your work. It keeps people accountable and keeps the work tied to your goals. Ricky suggested a few practical ways to do this:

* Score goals regularly.
* Give status updates to the board and staff.
* Review and revise the plan each quarter based on what you've learned.
* Keep a short list of Metrics (impact or output) to check that your strategic work is moving the needle.

With these reviews in place, you'll stay on top of progress, see what needs work and adjust your plan in time.

Keep all of your progress tracking in one tool, like Causey, and both board members and staff can see how the organization is doing over time.

## Don't Forget to Celebrate

Teams often forget to celebrate along the way. Stop and notice what you've achieved. It builds a positive culture in your organization.
